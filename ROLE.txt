ROLE
You are a Senior UI/UX Developer with 20 years of experience in service-ticketing,
campus and facility-management products. Users are tired, busy students who just want
a broken tap or fan fixed, wardens who are overloaded, and maintenance staff working
from phones. Every decision must reduce effort, make progress visible and build trust
that complaints never get lost.

PROJECT: "Fix It Fast: Hostel Maintenance Complaints"
Problem: complaints about plumbing, electrical and cleaning issues get lost, and
residents cannot see progress. Build a ticketing app where residents file complaints
with room, category, priority and photo; wardens assign tickets to maintenance staff;
staff post status updates on a ticket timeline; residents are notified, confirm the
resolution and rate the service; and a warden dashboard highlights pending and overdue
tickets.

STEP 0: SETUP
Clone and read the README of https://github.com/viettranx/3dviz-pro-max.git.
Use 3dviz-pro-max to build the 3D scene. Follow its real API. Do not invent functions.
State any assumption.

1. CREATIVE CONCEPT: "The Hostel Quarter"
A small explorable 3D low-poly hostel campus at twilight is the home screen and the
navigation metaphor. Warm lit windows show active tickets. Calm, friendly, never
childish. The UI layer on top is monochrome liquid glass so it never fights the scene.

Buildings (each maps to a feature):
- Hostel Block: residents file a new complaint (room, category, priority, photo)
- Notice Board Tower: "My tickets" list and the ticket timeline
- Warden's Office: warden dashboard (pending, overdue, assignments)
- Workshop: maintenance staff queue and status updates
- Utility Yard: category zones (plumbing tap, electrical bulb, cleaning broom)
- Clock Tower: overdue and SLA timers (it glows or chimes softly when tickets are overdue)
- Feedback Gazebo: confirm resolution and rate the service
- Main Gate: login and role selection (Resident / Warden / Staff)

Live data in the scene: each hostel window lights amber for open tickets and dims when
resolved; the Clock Tower shows the overdue count. Click any building to open its glass
panel.

Creatures (at least 3, subtle and looping): campus cats wandering paths, fireflies near
the active building, a small maintenance drone or birds circling the Clock Tower. All
respect prefers-reduced-motion.

2. BACKGROUND: "Twilight Valley" 3D wallpaper
Layered, depth-rich, calm and never busy. Back to front: sky gradient with faint stars
and a soft low moon (Obsidian) or pale sunrise haze (Frost); slow low-contrast aurora;
3 to 4 misty mountain silhouette layers; mid hills with tiny lantern lights; drifting
volumetric fog; sparse fireflies away from text areas; soft foreground vignette.
Palette: monochrome base (deep indigo/charcoal for Obsidian, soft white/grey for Frost)
plus one muted amber accent. No bright or saturated colours.
Motion: per-layer parallax on mouse move, device tilt and dock navigation; when a
building is selected, the background dims and blurs slightly (depth of field).
Glass compatibility: keep the area behind panels low in contrast and detail; add an

Fallbacks: prefers-reduced-motion freezes parallax, fog and particles; low-end devices,
no WebGL or 2D mode use pre-rendered WebP/AVIF wallpapers (16:9 desktop, 9:16 mobile)
with a plain gradient as the last fallback. Theme toggle cross-fades Frost and Obsidian.

3. DESIGN LANGUAGE: LIQUID GLASS (every button, card, input, chip, modal, toast,
   tooltip, ticket card and timeline item)
References: https://liquidglassdesign.com/ui ("Stepper design", "Liquid Glass Button",
"iOS 26 Liquid Glass Buttons", "LB Frosted Glass Card Design", "Notifications").
Recipe:
- Translucent fill (white 8-18% light, 6-12% dark) + backdrop-filter: blur(16-28px)
  saturate(140-180%)
- 1px gradient border with a top-left specular highlight, plus inset top glow
- Soft, large, low-opacity shadows only; radii 20-32px, pill controls, no sharp edges
- Optional SVG displacement filter on large panels for a liquid bend
- Monochrome Frost (soft whites/greys) and Obsidian (dark charcoal, subtle highlights);
  desaturated accents for status only
- Press: scale .97 with spring easing; hover: light sweep
- Fallbacks: @supports not (backdrop-filter) and prefers-reduced-transparency use solid
  opaque surfaces. Text on glass always keeps 4.5:1 contrast.
- Status and priority must never rely on colour alone: always pair colour with an icon
  and a text label (Open, Assigned, In progress, Resolved, Reopened, Overdue; Low,
  Medium, High, Urgent).

Toggle style "Frost" (dock, wide switches): minimalist toggle switch, soft
glassmorphism, frosted glass with subtle shadows, gentle lighting, soft whites and
greys, ample negative space, serene modern mood. Negative: low contrast, harsh shadows,
clutter, bright colors, excessive detail, text-heavy elements, sharp edges, watermarks.
--ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 250
Toggle style "Obsidian" (small buttons and switches): sleek mobile toggle, liquid glass
with frosted and glossy materials, soft ambient light, dark monochrome with subtle
highlights, depth, sophisticated mood. Negative: low contrast, harsh shadows, clutter,
bright colors, jagged edges, readability issues, watermarks, excessive detail.
--ar 1:1 --style raw --stylize 250

4. NAVIGATION: NO NAVBAR. ROLE-AWARE BOTTOM TOGGLE DOCK.
- Floating glass dock fixed at bottom centre (safe-area aware) with a segmented toggle
  (icons + short labels; labels collapse to icons on small screens). Segments change by
  role, 5 max:
  Resident: Home, New Ticket, My Tickets, Alerts, Profile
  Warden: Overview, Tickets, Staff, Reports, Settings
  Maintenance staff: My Jobs, Updates, History
- One liquid-glass thumb slides behind the active segment with spring easing
  (~350-450ms), stretching slightly mid-travel like a droplet, then settling
- role="tablist", arrow-key navigation, visible focus ring, 44px+ targets, aria labels,
  and a numeric badge on Alerts and Overdue
- Switching a segment flies the 3D camera to the matching building and slides a glass
  panel up above the dock
- The dock shrinks while the user types and returns on scroll or tap
- Theme (Frost/Obsidian), text size, high contrast, language and notification settings
  live in a glass Settings sheet opened from a small round glass toggle beside the dock

5. CORE FEATURES AND UI (all liquid glass)
Resident:
- New ticket (one question per step): room number (with hostel block), category
  (Plumbing, Electrical, Cleaning, Other) with large icon chips, priority (Low, Medium,
  High, Urgent) with a short hint of what each means, description, photo upload (camera
  or gallery, compressed, multiple photos, preview and remove), review and submit
- Duplicate check: warn if an open ticket already exists for the same room and category
- My tickets: glass cards with ID, category icon, priority, status chip, age, assigned
  staff; filters and search
- Ticket detail: a vertical TIMELINE with each status update (who, when, note, photo),
  expected completion time, and a "Nudge warden" action when overdue
- Notifications: toast plus an Alerts panel for assigned, in progress and resolved
- Confirm resolution: "Is it fixed?" with Yes, or No (reopen with a reason and photo)
- Rating: 1 to 5 stars plus an optional comment after confirming
Warden:
- Dashboard: summary glass tiles (Pending, In progress, Overdue, Resolved this week,
  average resolution time, average rating), an Overdue list pinned at the top, charts
  by category and by hostel block
- Ticket table or board: filters (status, category, priority, block), sort by age
- Assign: pick staff by skill and current workload, set due time; reassign; bulk
  assign
- Overdue logic: SLA by priority (e.g. Urgent 4h, High 24h, Medium 48h, Low 72h),
  configurable, with escalation highlight
Maintenance staff:
- My jobs: list sorted by priority and due time; open a job, post status updates
  (Started, Waiting for parts, Resolved) with a note and an optional photo; each update
  appends to the timeline
Global states: loading skeletons, empty states ("No tickets, all fixed!"), error and
offline states (queue a ticket drafted offline and sync later), success toasts, modals
for confirmations.

6. STATUS FLOW
Open -> Assigned -> In progress -> (Waiting for parts) -> Resolved (by staff) ->
Closed (resident confirms) or Reopened (resident rejects). Every transition writes a
timeline entry and sends a notification.

7. UX PRINCIPLES (non-negotiable)
- Filing a ticket takes under 60 seconds and 5 taps on mobile
- Plain, friendly language; the resident always knows the status and the next step
- Never lose a complaint: autosave drafts, confirm submission with a ticket ID
- Mobile-first for residents and staff; dense but calm dashboard on desktop for wardens
- WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard navigation, screen reader labels, contrast, focus states.
  Glass effects must never reduce readability.
- Full 2D non-3D fallback (list and dashboard views only) for low-end devices, no
  WebGL, or accessibility needs
- Lazy-load 3D, target 60fps, disable heavy blur on low-end devices
- Optional languages: English and Hindi (i18n files)

Supabase/Firebase) for tickets, users, roles and timeline; Web Push or in-app

11. ANIMATION SYSTEM: "THE COMPLAINT JOURNEY"
Everything in the product is animated with purpose. Animation must explain what is
happening (where the complaint is, who has it, what happens next), never decorate.

A. THE TICKET ORB (signature feature)
Every complaint becomes a small glowing liquid-glass orb (or sealed glass letter) that
physically travels through the 3D campus along curved paths (CatmullRom curves) with a
soft light trail. One orb per ticket; its colour and icon show the category (tap,
bulb, broom) and a ring shows priority.

Journey choreography, tied to the status flow:
1. SUBMIT: the form card folds into the orb, lifts out of the glass panel, and
   launches from the Hostel Block window (that window lights amber)
2. OPEN -> ASSIGNED: the orb flies to the Warden's Office, hovers in a "waiting
   tray" with a gentle pulse; the warden assigns it, and the orb splits a light
   thread to the chosen staff member
3. ASSIGNED -> IN PROGRESS: the orb travels along the path to the Workshop, then to
   the target room; a small tool animation (wrench, spark or water drop) loops above
   the room window while work is going on
4. WAITING FOR PARTS: the orb parks at the Utility Yard with a slow breathing pulse
   and a small clock icon
5. RESOLVED: the orb turns bright, flies back to the resident's window and bursts into
   soft light particles; the window dims to show it is no longer open
6. CONFIRM + RATE: a ribbon of light carries the orb to the Feedback Gazebo; stars fill
   in one by one when the resident rates
7. REOPENED: the orb reverses back along the path in a warm tint to the Warden's Office
8. OVERDUE: the orb's trail flickers amber and the Clock Tower pulses and chimes
   softly; the orb stays visible until action is taken

"Track my complaint" button on every ticket: the camera glides to the ticket's current
location, replays the path already travelled as a fading trail, and highlights where
it will go next with a dotted path. Dragging the 2D timeline scrubs the orb back and
forth along the path (timeline and 3D scene stay in sync).

B. TIMELINE ANIMATION (2D glass panel)
- The vertical line draws itself downward as events are added (stroke-dashoffset)
- Each new update slides in with a spring and a soft glow, then settles
- The status chip morphs (shared-element transition) from the old status to the new one
- A photo attached to an update expands from a thumbnail into a lightbox and back
- A live "typing / working" shimmer appears on the active step

C. SCREEN AND COMPONENT TRANSITIONS
- Dock: liquid thumb slides and stretches between segments (350-450ms spring)
- Panels slide up from the dock with a slight scale and blur-in; they close by
  collapsing back into the dock segment that opened them
- Shared-element transitions: a ticket card expands into the ticket detail view and
  collapses back (layout animations)
- Form steps: the current question slides out left, the next one slides in from the
  right; the progress stepper fills with a liquid wave; going back reverses the motion
- Category and priority chips: press squash (scale .97), selection ripples outward, a
  check morphs in
- Photo upload: the image shrinks and flies into a slot, with a circular progress ring
  around it while compressing and uploading
- Buttons: light sweep on hover, spring press, a loading spinner that morphs out of
  the label, a tick that morphs in on success
- Toasts and notifications: drop in from the top with a soft bounce; the bell icon
  swings once; the dock badge pops with a spring
- Lists and dashboards: staggered fade and rise on first load (40-60ms per item);
  counters count up; charts draw in; the overdue tile breathes gently
- Skeletons: soft shimmer; empty states: a small looping illustration (e.g. a sleeping
  cat on the notice board)
- Rating stars: fill with a pop and sparkle, one by one
- Theme switch Frost/Obsidian: circular reveal from the toggle with the wallpaper
  cross-fading

D. CAMPUS AMBIENT ANIMATION (already listed, keep subtle)
Window lights flicker softly, fog drifts, cats wander, fireflies orbit the active
building, the Clock Tower hands move, and the drone or birds circle the tower.

E. FIRST-TIME EXPLAINER
A skippable 20-second animated tour on first launch: a sample complaint orb travels
Hostel Block -> Warden's Office -> Workshop -> Gazebo while short glass captions appear
("You report", "Warden assigns", "Staff fixes", "You confirm and rate"). A "How it
works" replay button lives in the Settings sheet.

F. ANIMATION TOKENS AND RULES
- Durations: micro 120-200ms, UI 250-450ms, scene travel 1.5-3s per path leg
- Easing: springs (stiffness ~260, damping ~24) for UI; easeInOutCubic for camera and
  orb paths; no linear motion, nothing abrupt
- Animate only transform and opacity where possible; avoid animating blur radius on
  large areas
- Never more than 3 simultaneous attention-grabbing animations on screen
- prefers-reduced-motion: orbs teleport between stops with a quick fade, the timeline
  appears without sliding, parallax, creatures and fog are static, and the explainer
  becomes a still step-by-step slideshow
- Low-end devices or 2D mode: replace the 3D orb journey with a 2D animated progress
  track (a glass pill that slides across 5 labelled stations: Reported, Assigned, In
  progress, Resolved, Rated)
- Sound is optional and off by default (soft chime for overdue and resolved)
- Every animation must work offline and must not delay actions: users can interrupt or
  skip any animation without losing input

G. TECH FOR ANIMATION
Framer Motion for UI and layout animations; GSAP (or Three.js tweens with
CatmullRomCurve3 and TubeGeometry trails) for 3D orb paths and camera moves; a
central AnimationController that listens to ticket status events and triggers the
matching orb leg, timeline entry, window light and notification together, so
animation always reflects real data. Provide a small dev panel to replay each stage.
